# Spirit Week page: direction contract (v3, 25 Sep)

History. v1 (four-ink risograph on white paper, old cut-out athletes as flat ink plates) was rejected: "too torn apart, flat and dry, the photos have no impact". v2 (night stadium, one pole vault in WebGL colour trails) was rejected: "ugly, the layout does not show the order of the days; v1's day layout was fine, the problem is the pictures; use the current Olympics, different sports; when I scroll, the athletes should move, frozen until then, like scrubbing a video".

THESIS: Scroll is the play button. Four athletes from Paris 2024, four sports, four house colours. Every athlete is a real frame sequence cut out of real footage; the page's scroll position is the playhead, so they are frozen mid-action until the reader scrolls, then throw, lunge, flip and shoot exactly as fast as the reader scrolls, and stop when the reader stops.

WORLD: ink ground #0E0F13; house colours as flat, saturated fields: yellow #FFC21A, blue #2F6BFF, red #FF3B30, green #17C964; text #F4F5F8 on ink, ink on yellow/red/green, white on blue. Display: Anybody black italic, condensed; text: Barlow. Sharp corners, no cards, no glows.

FIRST VIEWPORT (pinned for about a screen and a half of scroll): four skewed house-colour slabs on the right, like lanes leaning into a bend, one athlete on each, overlapping their neighbours: discus (yellow), sabre (blue), BMX (red), 3x3 basketball (green). SPIRIT WEEK huge on the left with the dates, lunchtime and the courtyard. A thin scrub bar along the bottom fills as the athletes play.

THE WEEK: v1's lanes, kept on Steve's word. One full-width lane per day, top to bottom, Monday to Thursday: date, the game in huge type, and that day's athlete on the right breaking out of the top of the lane. Each lane's athlete plays while the lane crosses the screen.

Motion rule: scroll position drives every frame; nothing autoplays. Reduced motion: no pinning, each athlete shown at its peak frame.

Content rule (Steve): every fact from the school's poster, the ManageBac post and teachers' emails; no invented words. The sports in the pictures do not have to match the games. Athlete captions give name and sport only.
